# Shot check

`plan_shot.py` owns the prompt text. This note is the measured reason for those pins. It is not a second procedure.

On 2026-10-25, one paper boat was generated twice in a busy stream and twice on open water, plus earlier single takes of other wordings. Clips were 6 seconds, 720p, 16:9. Background difference is the mean absolute difference of non-red pixels between the first and last sampled frames. End match is the full-frame difference against the staged end still.

Pinned first and last frames, with the lock sentence, kept the stream (background difference about 10) and matched the end still (about 2). The same travel sentence without those pins, and without "stay fixed", replaced the shot (background difference about 46 to 55). Saying the banks stay fixed, without an end pin, also kept the stream (about 10) but did not land on a prepared end still.

A middle still whose pose faced the camera made the path even and turned the boat. A prompt that named mist and leaves and no destination left the boat near the center.

Open water kept the boat's size steadier than the stream and matched an end still less tightly, because the water has no landmark.

These figures are one object, two backgrounds, and two repeats of the pinned and locked-only methods. They are not a person, a line of speech, or a fast action.
